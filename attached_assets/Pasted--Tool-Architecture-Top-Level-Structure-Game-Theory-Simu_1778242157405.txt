4. Scenario Library (12 Core Scenarios)
Organized into four strategic domains. Each scenario includes a narrative wrapper, a decision interface, and a post-decision debrief with game theory concept reinforcement.

DOMAIN A: COOPERATION & CONFLICT
A1. The Prisoner's Dilemma (Classic)

Premise: Two suspects are interrogated separately. Each can cooperate with the other (stay silent) or defect (betray). The outcome depends on both choices.
Mechanic: Simultaneous choice — Cooperate or Defect. No communication.
Payoff Matrix: Standard PD matrix (displayed as years of sentence)
Concept: Dominant strategy, Nash Equilibrium, tragedy of the commons
Difficulty: Entry

A2. Repeated Prisoner's Dilemma

Premise: Same scenario but over 10 rounds. The opponent's history is visible. You can build a reputation or exploit one.
Mechanic: Sequential rounds. Opponent uses one of four AI strategies (Tit-for-Tat, Always Defect, Grim Trigger, Random). Strategy is hidden until debrief.
Concept: Folk theorem, reputation effects, shadow of the future
Difficulty: Intermediate

A3. The Stag Hunt

Premise: Two hunters can pursue a stag together (high reward, requires both) or each hunt a rabbit alone (guaranteed small reward). Neither knows what the other will do.
Mechanic: Simultaneous choice. No communication before round 1; limited signal possible in round 2.
Concept: Coordination game, payoff-dominant vs. risk-dominant equilibria
Difficulty: Entry


DOMAIN B: BARGAINING & NEGOTIATION
B1. Ultimatum Game

Premise: You receive $100. You propose how to split it with a counterpart. They accept or reject. If they reject, neither gets anything.
Mechanic: Slider input for offer (0-100). AI counterpart has a hidden rejection threshold. User must infer it through pattern or principle.
Concept: Subgame perfect equilibrium, fairness norms, behavioral deviation
Difficulty: Entry

B2. The Nash Bargaining Problem

Premise: Two parties are negotiating terms of a deal. Both have a walk-away (BATNA) value. The deal space is displayed as a frontier. Players submit positions and concede incrementally.
Mechanic: Multi-round offer/counter-offer with a visible Pareto frontier. Timer creates pressure.
Concept: Nash Bargaining Solution, Pareto efficiency, disagreement points
Difficulty: Advanced

B3. Divide the Surplus

Premise: Three-player negotiation. Any two can form a coalition and split the value, leaving the third out. Coalitions can shift each round.
Mechanic: Coalition formation interface. Players bid for coalition membership.
Concept: Coalition games, core, Shapley value
Difficulty: Advanced


DOMAIN C: COMPETITION & MARKETS
C1. The Pricing War

Premise: Two firms set prices for a homogeneous product each quarter. Consumers go to the lower price. High prices mean higher margins but lost share.
Mechanic: Price slider per round (8 rounds). Opponent AI uses one of three pricing strategies (aggressive, cooperative, reactive).
Concept: Bertrand competition, collusion incentives, market equilibrium
Difficulty: Intermediate

C2. The Cournot Duopoly

Premise: Two firms decide how much to produce. Market price falls as total supply rises. Each firm maximizes its own profit.
Mechanic: Quantity selector per round. Payoff curve updates dynamically as decisions are entered.
Concept: Cournot equilibrium, reaction functions, best response
Difficulty: Intermediate

C3. The Auction

Premise: You are bidding in a sealed-bid second-price auction. Your private value for the item is shown. Other bidders' values are hidden.
Mechanic: Enter your bid. Outcome reveals all bids and explains why the dominant strategy is truthful bidding.
Concept: Vickrey auction, dominant strategy in mechanism design, winner's curse (in a variant)
Difficulty: Entry


DOMAIN D: SIGNALING & INFORMATION
D1. The Signaling Game

Premise: You are a job applicant. Education is costly but signals ability. Employers offer wages based on signals. Do you invest in the credential?
Mechanic: Two-stage decision. Stage 1: invest in signal (yes/no, with displayed cost). Stage 2: wage offer reveals whether it was worth it. Employer type (sophisticated vs. naive) is randomized.
Concept: Spence signaling model, separating vs. pooling equilibria
Difficulty: Advanced

D2. The Bluffing Game

Premise: Simplified poker variant. You are dealt a hand (strong/weak, shown only to you). You can bet or fold. The opponent can call or fold. Outcome depends on hands and choices.
Mechanic: 5-round game. Track bluff frequency. Post-round, opponent's strategy adapts.
Concept: Mixed strategy Nash Equilibrium, credible threats, information asymmetry
Difficulty: Intermediate

D3. The Cheap Talk Game

Premise: You are an expert (informed) sending a recommendation to a decision-maker (uninformed). Your interests may or may not be aligned. The decision-maker must decide whether to follow your advice.
Mechanic: Role assignment per round (sender/receiver alternates). Alignment is probabilistic and hidden.
Concept: Crawford-Sobel model, babbling equilibrium, strategic communication
Difficulty: Advanced


5. Core Mechanics & Interaction Design
5A. Session Flow
Scenario Hub
    → User selects scenario (or "Surprise Me" assigns one)
    → Scenario Brief loads (narrative + context)
    → Decision Interface activates
    → User makes choice(s)
    → Outcome Engine resolves
    → Payoff Matrix fully revealed
    → Debrief panel: concept name, explanation, how this round's result illustrates it
    → Score updates, concept logged
    → Option: Replay / Next Scenario / Return to Hub
5B. Decision Interfaces (per scenario type)
Interface TypeUsed InDescriptionBinary choice buttonsA1, A3, B1 (accept/reject)Two styled action buttons, full-width, labeled clearlySlider inputB1 (offer), C1 (price), C2 (quantity)Draggable range with live value display in DM MonoMulti-round sequentialA2, B2, C1, C2, D2Turn-based flow with round counter and history logCoalition selectorB3Drag-to-group or checkbox coalition builderNumeric bid entryC3Input field with private value displayed prominentlyTwo-stage branchingD1Step 1 and Step 2 presented sequentially with outcome bridge
5C. Payoff Matrix Display
Every scenario surfaces a payoff matrix. Rules:

Matrix is partially obscured before the decision is made (opponent values hidden)
After decision, full matrix reveals with animation (cells flip or fade in)
Player's outcome highlighted in burnt orange
All values displayed in DM Mono
Matrix labeled clearly: rows = Player 1 strategy, columns = Player 2 strategy

5D. Opponent AI Behaviors
Each scenario's AI opponent operates on one of the following logic types (hidden until debrief):

Rational/Theoretical: Plays the Nash Equilibrium strategy always
Reactive: Mirrors player's last move (Tit-for-Tat style)
Exploitative: Identifies and defects on cooperative patterns
Noisy: Plays a mixed strategy with randomization
Behavioral: Incorporates fairness norms and non-rational preferences

The AI type is revealed in the debrief with an explanation of what it implies for real-world counterpart modeling.

6. Scoring & Progression System
Score Architecture

Points per round: Determined by outcome relative to Nash Equilibrium benchmark

Matched or beat the equilibrium prediction: +100 base
Deviated but achieved higher joint payoff: +75 + "Cooperation Bonus" badge
Suboptimal outcome: +25 + feedback note


Streak multiplier: Consecutive correct/optimal decisions multiply score (1x, 1.5x, 2x, 2.5x)
Concept mastery: First time each concept is encountered, a badge is awarded and logged

Progress Indicators

Scenario Hub shows completion state per card (untouched / in progress / complete / mastered)
A "Concepts Encountered" counter tracks unique game theory concepts surfaced
An "Insight Log" records one key takeaway per completed scenario, phrased as a strategic principle

Difficulty Scaling

Entry: Pre-set AI strategy, explained payoff matrix, guided debrief
Intermediate: AI type hidden, matrix partially labeled, less guided debrief
Advanced: AI type unknown, matrix unlabeled, user must name the concept encountered


7. Debrief Panel Design
After every scenario resolution, the Debrief Panel slides in from the right (or expands below on mobile). It contains:

Outcome Summary — What happened, stated plainly (e.g., "Both players defected. Total payoff: 4 years combined sentence.")
Optimal Path — What the Nash Equilibrium predicted and why
Concept Name + Definition — E.g., "Dominant Strategy: a choice that produces the best outcome regardless of what the opponent does."
Real-World Anchor — One sentence connecting the concept to a real strategic context (business, geopolitics, negotiation)
Your Score This Round — Displayed in DM Mono with streak indicator
Replay / Next — Two CTA buttons


8. Navigation & Layout Principles
Scenario Hub

Card-based grid (3 columns desktop, 1 column mobile)
Each card: scenario title, domain badge (color-coded by domain A/B/C/D), difficulty chip, one-line premise, completion state
Filter bar: All / Cooperation / Bargaining / Competition / Signaling
"Surprise Me" button assigns a random incomplete scenario

Active Simulation

Two-panel layout: Left = scenario context + opponent info. Right = decision interface + payoff matrix.
Mobile: stacked, context above, interface below
Persistent: round counter, current score, streak indicator in header strip

Scoreboard / Insight Log

Clean table: Scenario | Domain | Score | Concept Mastered | Date
Insight Log: chronological list of strategic principles, phrased in first person ("I learned that in repeated games, reputation shapes equilibrium more than rational calculation alone.")


9. Copy Tone Standards

Scenario titles: declarative and specific ("The Pricing War", not "Price Competition Game")
Debrief copy: analytical, direct, no hedging. State what happened and why.
UI labels: lowercase where appropriate, never all-caps except domain badges
Error/empty states: brief and purposeful ("No scenarios completed yet. Start with The Prisoner's Dilemma.")
No em dashes throughout. Use colons, commas, or new sentences.


10. Technical Implementation Notes
Recommended Stack

Single-file HTML artifact for initial build (consistent with STEEP tool pattern on Vercel)
Vanilla JS for state management and game logic (no framework dependency for simplicity)
CSS custom properties for theming (use STINT.Studio palette tokens)
Google Fonts: Cormorant Garamond (400, 600, 700) + DM Mono (400, 500) + Instrument Sans (400, 500)

State Management Requirements

Current scenario state (round, choices made, scores)
Session history (scenarios completed, concepts encountered, scores)
AI opponent behavior state (type assigned, history of moves)
Persisted via localStorage between sessions

Animation Guidelines

Payoff matrix cell reveals: CSS transition, staggered by 50ms per cell
Score ticker: countUp animation on score update (DM Mono, burnt orange)
Decision buttons: subtle press feedback (scale transform)
Debrief panel: slide-in from right, 300ms ease-out
No particle effects, no heavy canvas animations

Accessibility

All decision interfaces keyboard-navigable
Color never the sole indicator of state (pair with label/icon)
Minimum 4.5:1 contrast on all text
Focus states visible and styled (burnt orange outline)
